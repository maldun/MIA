# Spikes of the skill rework (throwaway, not eval runs)

## Spike C: LightRAG extraction on the 12B (run 2026-11-04)
- **Setup:** `lightrag-hku` 1.5.7 in a throwaway venv (Python 3.12, outside `requirements.txt`); Ollama with `gemma4:12b-it-qat`, `num_ctx` 8192, temperature 0, thinking off (with thinking on the 12B spent its whole output budget on reasoning and returned nothing); chunks of 800 tokens, no gleaning; embeddings are deterministic hash vectors (the server answers 501 for embeddings and no embedding model is pulled), so **nothing is known about LightRAG's retrieval side**.
- **Documents:** 10 pages fetched from the web (8 Wikipedia articles capped at 12 000 characters: citation, knowledge graph, meta-analysis, ontology, peer review, prompt injection, RAG, systematic review; abstracts of the LightRAG and original RAG papers). 3 repeats each. Scripts: `spikes/lightrag/`.
- **Measured:** 30 runs, 0 errors, 37 min in all. Pages of about 12 000 characters: 70 to 130 s, 45 to 80 nodes and 23 to 67 edges; abstracts: 14 to 20 s, 8 to 12 nodes. At temperature 0 the repeats give nearly the same node counts, so repeats add little; more documents add more.
- **Observed:** several results have fewer edges than nodes (e.g. 77 nodes, 40 edges): isolated entities, to check in the marking.
- **Still open:** the quality (right, useless, wrong, invented), judged by the owner on a sample of 195 items with their source passages: `forms/spike-c-marking.html`. The bar for "good enough" was set by the owner before reading the result (below).
- **Not compared yet:** option 1 (own builder on the runner) on the same documents. Needs the owner's marking first.

**Bar for spike C (owner, 2026-11-05, set before the marking):** at least 68 % of the marked items *right* (one sigma) and at most 5 % *invented*. Agent's reading, to confirm: the shares are of all marked items; "right but useless" and "wrong" do not count as right and are reported on their own; the missing-items note per document is reported but does not decide. Option 1 is run on the same documents against the same bar after the marking.

## Spike C, first opinion and hard checks (2026-11-05)
- **Agent's blind labels** (not truth labels, `spikes/lightrag/judge-claude.json`, made before the owner's marking, read against the full documents): 168 right (86.2 %), 22 right but useless (11.3 %), 3 wrong (1.5 %), 2 invented (1.0 %). Errors: a name cut from "PRIMSA flow diagram" (2 items), two edges inferred, not stated (Boolean operators with PubMed and Embase), one reversed edge (Bluebook). Useless items are mostly generic nouns, example details and duplicates.
- **Chunker finding:** LightRAG cuts chunks at token borders, mid-word: "PRIMSA" was split into "PRIM" and "SA flow diagram", so a check against the *chunk* passes the mangled name. Our own builder must chunk at sentence or paragraph borders and check against the whole document.
- **Hard checks** (`spikes/lightrag/checks.py`, test `test_checks.py`, measurement `measure_checks.py`; the plural handling was added after the first measurement showed two false flags from plurals, so the numbers below are for the corrected check, tuned on a failing unit test and not on the labels):

| Check | Flagged of 195 | Catches of the 5 wrong/invented | False flags on 168 "right" |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: name or both ends in the source chunk | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| B: relation ends within 300 characters in the chunk | 17 | 0 | 14 |
| C: as B, against the whole document | 18 | 2 | 14 |
| **D: every name occurs in the whole document (no distance rule)** | **3** | **2** | **0** |

  Over all 818 items of repeat 1, check D flags 9 of 494 entities (1.8 %) and 9 of 324 relations (2.8 %). The distance rule (B, C) flags correct relations that span sentences and is not worth it. D is cheap and has no false flags here, but it **cannot** catch a reversed or inferred relation (I015, I057, I059): those need the source quote of the relation plus a judge (model) that asks whether the quote states the relation, calibrated against the owner.
- **Reading:** on the agent's labels LightRAG on the 12B clears the owner's bar by a wide margin on right and invented. This is an opinion until the owner's marks exist; the agreement of the two sets decides whether a model can serve as a judge (kappa >= 0.6).

## Spike (a) result: verify.cops and verify.jury as debates on the 12B (2026-11-05)
Throwaway `spikes/debate/debate_spike.py` (loop tests green with a scripted model), raw rows `spikes/debate/run1.jsonl`. `gemma4:12b-it-qat`, num_ctx 8192, temperature 0.7, seeds 1 to 3, caps 3 rounds / 12 calls / 300 s. **10 own claims** (4 near-miss false numbers or dates, 3 true numbers, 1 myth, 2 unverifiable), truth set by the coding agent for this spike only; 90 runs in about 8 minutes. Not an eval run, not eval cases.

| Variant | Right | False pass (non-true claim passed) | False fail | Seconds per claim (mean) | Model calls (mean) | Rounds |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| single | 21 of 30 | 7 | 2 | 1.4 | 1.0 | 1 |
| cops as a debate | 23 of 30 | 4 | 2 | 8.1 | 4.2 | 1 in 23 runs, 3 (cap) in 6, 1 run failed (cut-off JSON) |
| jury as a debate | **29 of 30** | **0** | 1 | 7.3 | 4.6 | 1 in 17 runs, 2 in 10, 3 (cap) in 3 |

- **The jury debate fixed the near-miss numbers** that a single voice passes: Burj Khalifa, Everest 8,948 m and Moon 284,000 km were refuted in 3 of 3 runs (single: 0, 1 and 1 of 3). Disagreement in round 1 was resolved after the experts saw each other's reasons; 29 of 30 debates ended in agreement. The old one-shot jury changed nothing (`eval/baselines/verdicts-styles.md`), so the rebuttal round is what helps, not the three personas alone.
- **The cops debate helped only on the Burj Khalifa** and ran to the round cap on the near-miss claims where the judge never became sure; the old one-shot cops had fixed all three near-miss numbers, so this cops form is not better than the existing one.
- Both debate forms stayed far below the caps: at most 9 calls and well under a minute per claim in this run; the 12-call and 5-minute caps never fired (the budget path is covered by the unit test only).
- The true claim "Everest is about 8,849 m" is the one error left (single 1 of 3, jury 2 of 3): the 12B does not know the figure reliably; that is a knowledge gap, not a debate failure.
- **Caution:** 10 claims of which 4 are near-miss numbers, 3 seeds per claim are not independent, and the whole gain rests on three claims, as in the old styles baseline. This shows that the debate form works on the 12B within the caps; it does not rank the styles. The numbers 3 rounds / 12 calls / 5 minutes stay as defaults; the measured need is one to two rounds and about 5 calls.
- Seen on the way: one cops run failed on a model reply cut off in the middle of a JSON string; the debate step needs a retry or a fallback for malformed replies (a requirement for Plan).

## Spike C, option 1b: stricter entity rules (2026-11-11; `spikes/lightrag/option1b.py`; run and result below)
- **Why:** in the owner's marks of LightRAG, entities are the weak spot: 12 of 15 invented and 18 of 116 bad entities (15.5 %), relations 6 of 79 (7.6 %). All 24 invented or wrong items have names that occur in the document, so the name check cannot catch them; repeat stability does not either (bad items 14 % unstable, 12 % stable).
- **Rules (hard checks, no extra model call):** (1) the quote of an entity is one whole sentence of the document and contains the name (the "contains the name" part is option 1 already); (2) the entity has a `description` and every content word of it (more than 3 letters, no stop word, plural `s` dropped) occurs in its quote, so the description only restates the quote. `max_ungrounded` is the number of outside words allowed; **pre-set to 0 before the run**, other limits are read off the stored counts of the run and are not tuned on the marks. Relations are checked as in option 1.
- **Offline measurement on LightRAG's descriptions (`measure_grounding.py`, 116 marked entities, no model):** invented entities have more outside words than right ones, but LightRAG's free-text descriptions are not restating ones, so the rule is too harsh on them (limit 0 refuses 66 of 94 right entities, limit 2 refuses 19 of 94 and keeps 5 of 12 invented). It shows the signal exists; it does not show that option 1b works.
- **Run (2026-11-11, `option1b-run/`, minutes 20.0, no failed model step):** `gemma4:12b-it-qat` (digest `38044be4f923`), `num_ctx` 8192, temperature 0, `think` off, the 10 spike C documents, `max_ungrounded` 0. First validator: 444 accepted (305 entities, 139 relations), 114 refused (71 not one whole sentence, 37 quote lacks the name or an end, 6 quote not in the document, **0 for ungrounded description**).
- **Finding 1: rule 2 does nothing.** 299 of 305 accepted descriptions are word for word the quote: the model copies the sentence when told to restate it, so every description is grounded by construction and the limit 0 to 3 gives the same 305 entities. Rule 2 as a lexical check cannot help with this prompt.
- **Finding 2: rule 1 had a bug, fixed with a test (`test_option1b.py`).** The sentence splitter glued a wiki heading ("== Concept ==") to the next sentence, so correct quotes were refused (71 refusals). The rule now checks the quote ends like a sentence, holds no border inside and starts where a sentence starts in the document; the stored proposals were re-validated without a model (`revalidate_option1b.py`, `option1b-run-fixed/`): **482 accepted (343 entities, 139 relations), 76 refused (33 not one whole sentence, 37 name or ends, 6 quote not in the document)**. The 33 left are partly right (list lines cut off) and partly splitter artefacts (an abbreviation such as "Janice R. Walker" counts as a border); not tuned further.
- **Against option 1 (same documents):** of its 475 accepted entities, 321 are accepted again, 130 are not proposed again (the changed prompt gives other output), 24 are refused (23 by rule 1, 1 by the name rule).
- **Against the owner's marks (`analyze_option1b.py`):** of the marked items that 1b proposes again, rule 1 accepts 36 of 38 right and both "useless" ones and refuses 2 right ones (a cost of 5 %). **No gain can be seen: option 1 has no marked invented item (0 of 120), so there is nothing for the rules to catch.** 29 right and 2 useless marks are not proposed again, so they cannot be compared.
- **Reading:** option 1b adds no measurable gain over option 1 on the data we have. Rule 2 is empty with this prompt; rule 1 costs about 5 % of right items and has nothing to catch. The weak spot named above (entities of LightRAG) is not a weak spot of option 1. The agent's recommendation: **keep option 1** with the sentence-border chunker and the neighbour rule, drop rule 2, and look at rule 1 again only if a larger marked sample of option 1 shows invented entities.
- **Not done:** the new marking sample (`make_marking_option1b.py`, ids `P`) was not given to the owner: it would only measure option 1b against the 0 of 120 that option 1 already has.
- **Caveats:** the marked sets belong to other runs, so only items that 1b proposes again can be compared; a lexical check cannot see a description that is built from the quote's words but says something else; the limits are guesses until the sample is marked.
